# Result-neutral manuscript outline

**Provisional title:** Forecasting dwarf-nova recurrence: completed-outburst
information, changing activity and incomplete observation.

This is a structure for an eventual paper, not a draft report of results. No
astronomical analysis, effect size, p-value or sample size has been supplied.

## Abstract

Write last. State the prior problem, explicitly defined monitored sample,
prediction contrast, observation treatment, achieved result with uncertainty,
and the scope of the conclusion. Do not equate incremental prediction with a
unique accretion mechanism or describe historical memory correlations as new.

## 1. Introduction

Physical context and why recurrence forecasts can inform variability studies.
Historical SS Cyg and multi-system work; recent changes of state and prediction
approaches. Define the unresolved comparison precisely. State the central
question and the distinction between predictive and mechanistic claims.

## 2. Data and event definitions

Archive versions and queries, identities, initial cohort, exclusions and target
population. Time/passband/limit handling. Observation effort, gaps, blending,
saturation and duplication. Operational onset/end definitions, manual audit,
joint uncertainty and completeness validation. A transparent sample flow table.

## 3. Forecasting and simulation methods

As-of origins and completion confirmation; M0–M3 and their shared components;
censoring and interrupted episodes; inference and parameter uncertainty.
Common-calendar splits, scoring, star weighting, calibration and prespecified
sensitivity analyses. Simulations distinguish implementation, recovery,
identifiability and observation-policy robustness.

## 4. Results — placeholders only

1. Achieved data quality and available forecast episodes.
2. Reproduction and definition sensitivity.
3. Simulation operating characteristics and limits.
4. Paired held-out predictive comparisons and calibration.
5. Variation among systems/epochs; influence and observation sensitivity.
6. Structural identifiability, if actually tested; otherwise an explicit limit.

## 5. Discussion

What the comparison establishes, how it relates to prior claims, and which
physical interpretations remain compatible. Generalisability beyond monitored
episodes and selected systems. Useful negative results versus insufficient power.
Observation priorities implied by demonstrated uncertainty, if justified.
