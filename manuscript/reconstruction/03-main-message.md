# Step 1c: main message (24 words)

> **The star SS Cygni brightens in repeated outbursts; how long each lasted improved forecasts of
> when the next began, tested on decades of unseen observations.**

## Why this wording

- **A new reader learns what SS Cygni is and does,** what we found and how it was tested. The
  message assumes no knowledge of dwarf novae, scoring rules or preregistration.
- **It states the finding plainly.** Its size, the measurement assumptions and the single-star
  scope belong in the abstract's numbers and in one dedicated limitations section.
- **Past tense names the evaluated record** (Codex, 10 November 2026): the forecasts improved on
  SS Cyg's 1960–2017 record. "The star" tells a cold reader what SS Cygni is.
- **"Unseen"** means withheld from fitting and unopened until the forecasts were frozen and the
  protocol published. It does not claim an untouched evaluation, which C05 rules out; the parser
  repair is reported in the limitations.
- History: Barry found the first version caveat-laden and written for insiders (10 November 2026);
  he approved the second. This third version merges it with Codex's proposed wording.

## What the paper rests on

1. **The preregistered test.** Forecasts fitted on 1910–1959 were frozen, then scored on 1960–2017.
   Adding duration raised the score (C01).
2. **Measurement checks.** These show how the result changes when outbursts are timed in other ways
   (C44, C55).

The ten-star screen is a **transfer assessment** (C68, C70). It asks whether other dwarf novae can
support the same test and documents what a record needs for that. It is a separate question from
whether duration improves SS Cyg's forecasts, and the prose should present it as such.

## One paper

The measurement checks bound the main finding, and the transfer assessment says where the test can
be repeated; neither is a separate finding. Their limits are gathered in one place.

A general method for scoring forecasts against irregular citizen-science records, or a survey-era
multi-star study, would be a second paper.
