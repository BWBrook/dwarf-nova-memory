# Step 1b: abstract (under 150 words)

Built from `01-working-abstract.md`. 148 words.

> Dwarf-nova outbursts are the archetypal disc instability, yet whether a completed outburst helps
> forecast the next has been studied through in-sample correlations, not held-out forecasts. Using a
> century of AAVSO observations of SS Cygni, we fitted lognormal recurrence forecasts with and
> without outburst duration to 1910–1959, froze and preregistered them, and scored 1960–2017.
> Duration raised the held-out working score by 0.0165 nats per forecast (90% interval
> 0.0049–0.0281; 441 forecasts), meeting the preregistered criterion after a documented post-access
> parser repair. The score rests on extracted onset intervals, not a validated physical-onset model;
> measurement redesigns were positive but inconclusive. The gain is consistent with disc depletion or
> a slowly varying accretion state; shared measurement errors are not excluded. Under the same rules,
> none of ten other dwarf novae qualified for a comparable test. Long citizen-science records can
> support such tests; here, coverage and an unestablished observation model set the limits.

## Choices made to fit the length

- **Kept the parser repair (C05).** The estimands require every product that reports the primary
  result to say the run followed it.
- **Left out:** the two inconclusive predeclared secondaries (C07, C08), the rolling-adjustment
  sensitivity (C62), the simulations and the 1.7% translation of the gain (C02). Each belongs in the
  longer abstract or the results.
- **"Not held-out forecasts"** describes the duration question's literature (C40, C41). One prior
  held-out onset forecast exists, for a different star and without duration.
- **Last sentence.** It is descriptive of this study: the transfer screen's largest loss was coverage
  of the previous gap (C70), and the SS Cyg result lacks an established observation model. It is
  not a general law about citizen-science records.
