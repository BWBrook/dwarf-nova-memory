## R2 preparation qualification (D036)

The original C01 result is a specified working interval-score comparison; its
proper-latent-onset interpretation has not been established. D035 proposes the
latent visual crossing and new report windows. Codex's amended preparation
contract is `docs/protocols/r2-design-v1.md`; no new real R2b fit/score or product
rewrite is authorized in the R2a/c preparation execution.

Separate A0 (association/completeness/risk set), A1 (containment or genuine
censor survival), and A2 (the complete normalized CAR observation kernel).
The formal CAR theorem is conditional; a report margin or visit-rate diagnostic
does not prove its premises. Resolved-event inf/sup bounds are deterministic
conditional on containment. Censored survival contrasts are a different target
from an unknown latent point score. Fixed-cutoff mixed density/survival bounds
and the working lower-bound-backoff score policy must be named separately.
CR1 inference has additional dependence/coverage assumptions.

DF removes a literal interpolated end estimate from a new observed-clock rule;
it does not remove cadence-dependent anchor lag, shared reports or selection.
Positive/negative DF gains and retention ratios cannot identify a physical or
measurement mechanism. First decisive-faint availability may require a later
issue; comparisons across clocks require matched issue/outcome populations.
New rungs use a common stricter windowed report-candidate history and are not
the byte-identical original frozen M2. R2c score-level power is planning under
explicit synthetic assumptions, not X5 qualification or a measured archive count.

New R2 claims start provisional and need independent review. The four product
texts/PDFs stayed unchanged during preparation and review; D047 now integrates
the completed results. Later
real-data R2b execution and any new-star G3 proposal require their named source,
cohort, population and interpretation freezes; no new archive opening follows
from this preparation contract.

## R2b review qualification (D042, C61–C64)

**What changes.**
- B2 is the preselected comparator, with lower development likelihood and descriptive held-out
  baseline means than B0/B1 (C61). This motivates review of its windowed implementation, but
  does not uniquely identify the random-walk level as the cause or establish the latent gap
  process. H2 does not measure gain over the empirically best history model.
- Marks with era-shifted means can carry baseline calibration information. The post-result
  rolling guards attenuate observing-rate and fitted-duration gains (C62). They change both
  the baseline and predictor, so this is not a unique causal allocation. The analogous point-B0
  gain changes from 0.0184 to 0.0132; no fraction of C01 was measured.
- Historical "drift-guarded" diagnostics (C63) retain the original unguarded development beta,
  recalibrate from evaluation forecasts only, and use final saved outcome labels with proxy
  resolution times. Adding simple report-closure and coverage-recognition delays changes no
  SS Cyg update sets, but does not establish a complete prefix-causal label ledger. These
  diagnostics are not an executed replay of the proposed R2d predictor, which includes
  development history and its own guarded coefficient fit.
- Trailing mark centring and rolling level corrections attenuate specified slow variation;
  they do not exclude arbitrary slow co-variation by construction. Residual drift and
  observation pathways remain possible.
- C64 verifies a local Taylor relation and sample design effects, not prospective power.
  The predicted and observed score/gain ratios reuse the evaluation score and curvature.
  A score statistic for a zero working-coefficient moment and a test of positive gain from
  the actual development-fitted forecast address different nulls. Rejecting the former does
  not establish the latter; neither guarantees a physical mechanism or latent-score propriety.

**Allowed:**
- “The post-result guarded duration gains remain positive in SS Cyg. DF B0 and B2 gain
  intervals remain inconclusive; guarded point-original B0 has positive lower limits under
  both blockings. These are exploratory results.”
- “The observing-rate estimate also stays positive, with intervals spanning zero. This is
  compatible with an era-calibration pathway, without uniquely attributing its information.”
- “The analogous point-clock gain is smaller under the chosen rolling guards. The change is
  not a measured causal component of C01.”

**Not allowed:**
- “the observing-rate result shows a cadence artefact”;
- “B2 removing the gain shows a slow state”;
- “the guards remove all slow co-variation” or “the era shift explains the rate result”;
- a claim that about a third of C01 was causally attributed to the era shift;
- different significance labels used as evidence that duration and rate effects differ;
- a zero-coefficient score test described as confirmation of forecast gain;
- any post-result guarded statistic presented as a new confirmation;
- “adding the observing rate leaves the duration gain unchanged”: rate was fitted in place of
  duration; the 0.0043 duration gain is over a B2 base refitted with raw end cadence `c_end`
  (C56; D053). No joint rate-plus-duration fit exists;
- the guard reduction assigned to drift alone: it changes duration centring and baseline level
  together (D053).

## Proposed multi-star claims (D044, C67; not yet frozen)

**Sequence.** Association is tested first: whole-star score moment at the B0R base, guarded DF
span. Forecast gain (D018) is tested second, only if association rejects.
The retained R2d grid lost no gain rejections under this sequence; that is
empirical, not guaranteed future power. Strong familywise control requires both
tests valid under their respective full nulls, including positive association
with nonpositive frozen forecast gain. Pooled strict-oracle size is insufficient
to certify this. G3a approval concerns early-record feasibility; G3b remains closed.

**Allowed if both reject:**
- “the completed outburst's guarded span carries incremental information about the next
  quiescent interval beyond the level-corrected history baseline, and the development-trained
  forecast improves held-out working scores, in the qualifying cohort”.

**Allowed if only association rejects:**
- a positive guarded-span/working-baseline residual moment, with no demonstrated
  forecast improvement. Do not call this an efficient score or a model-free
  conditional-information result; nuisance and baseline adequacy matter.

**Not allowed:**
- mechanism or physical memory;
- generality beyond the qualifying cohort;
- a robust sample-size guarantee from the synthetic screen;
- association power cited as forecast-gain power;
- absence of an effect when association fails.

**Status after G3a (D046):** no cohort qualified (C68), so these claims are a design record for a
future cohort, not claims of this paper.

## Multi-star transfer (G3a, D046; C68–C71)

What G3a measured: whether ten catalogue-selected candidates' pre-1990 AAVSO
visual records support prospectively frozen extraction, timing and qualification
rules, published in Git before access. This was a development-record feasibility
screen, not a multi-star forecast-effect test. It computed no mark–gap relation.
Exclusions and attrition describe failure under those rules; they do not
identify a unique physical or observational cause.

**Allowed:**
- “under the frozen transfer rules, none of the ten candidates qualified for the planned
  multi-star test; SS Aur cleared the numerical support gates but triggered the operational
  long-bright-run screen”;
- descriptive ratios of supported issuances to confirmed-episode counts, with their
  time landmarks and raw versus derived hold-star streams named; do not present mixed
  numerators/denominators as a paired conversion probability;
- “in the ordinary raw-report diagnostic, the largest recorded attrition was the missing
  covered previous gap, including observing holes and ineligible onset windows”;
- “the tested gate deletions remained insufficient at the assumed development rates”;
- “the SS Cyg result is a single-star result; its generality is untested”.

**Not allowed:**
- “other dwarf novae show no memory”, or any statement about a duration effect in other stars;
- “the effect is unique to SS Cyg”;
- “the AAVSO archive cannot support any multi-star analysis”, or a claim about other archives or
  measurement models;
- “SS Aur shows standstills” or any subtype diagnosis from the morphology flag;
- “eclipses caused U Gem or IP Peg to fail”, or “the eclipse rule removed eclipse effects”;
- projected yields cited as observed counts, or gate relaxations presented as a pass;
- “SS Cyg's record times most successive outbursts” or a required completeness equal to SS Cyg's:
  the counts show enough supported, well-covered confirmed cycles under the frozen rules, not
  completeness against the physical event population (D053);
- the 490-forecast SS Cyg reference called development data: it spans the available pre-1990
  record, including 215 forecasts from 1960–1989 (D053).
- “the rules are not strict”, a causal attribution solely to visual depth, or a theorem
  that no new measurement model or later split could reach the target;
- “SS Aur had four physical outbursts in the flagged run” or “the contamination is
  photometric” without independent event/sequence evidence. Four separated decisive-bright
  report clusters and threshold-proximate visit medians are descriptive facts. The
  14.5-mag record-wide comparison is selected to be fainter than threshold and does
  not establish the true quiescent level in the flagged era.

## Primary forecast gain (C01–C04)

- **Estimand:** the mean paired log-score difference over the 441 SS Cyg issuances in 1960–2017.
  The target is the next extracted onset, conditional on the information at the observed
  end-of-outburst issue landmark. Frozen working lognormal clocks were fitted on 1910–1959 only.
  M2 uses the previous covered quiet gap and recent gap level; M3 adds current log duration.
  The result is conditional on archived `Vis.`/validation-`V` observations, X4 at magnitude ten,
  C1/seven-day coverage, score eligibility and observation-time replay. The interval uses CR1,
  six-year clusters and Student t with nine degrees of freedom.
- **Allowed:** “In a preregistered, frozen-parameter retrospective comparison, adding completed
  outburst duration raised the working interval score of SS Cyg next-onset forecasts by 0.0165 nats per issuance
  (90% interval 0.0049–0.0281; 441 issuances, 1960–2017). The predeclared rule was met.”
  “Incremental working-score gain beyond the specified measured-history timing baseline.”
  A proper physical-onset interpretation requires an observation model/premises that
  have not been established. Do not call the X4 fitted interval a hard observing
  bracket or a calibrated posterior without the required model justification.
- **Size in other units (C02):** exponentiating the mean log-score gain gives a geometric mean
  likelihood ratio, about 1.017. This can be called about 1.7% higher geometric mean likelihood
  for the scored evidence. It is not an accuracy percentage or the arithmetic mean probability
  gain. The ratio to M2's mean gain over M0 is about 14%; this is not variance explained.
- **Required reporting (D051):** Methods define the visual record, extraction, issue landmark,
  frozen timing baseline, working interval/survival score and retrospective information set.
  Results give the primary gain, uncertainty, population and predeclared decision, plus the
  later-period and type-rule outcomes (C07, C08). The limitations passage covers single-star scope,
  the unestablished physical-onset observation model, shared endpoint and observing pathways, the
  adequacy of the timing baseline (C10), the post-access format repair (C05), unknown historical
  upload/revision times and exploratory recalibration (C62). The analogous recalibration contrast
  changes model, score and population; it does not allocate a component of C01. Definitions and
  result identity remain necessary wherever they make a sentence intelligible.
- **Not allowed:** physical memory, causation, confirmation of a disc mechanism, generality to
  dwarf novae, operational forecasts, a blind/untouched evaluation, well-calibrated predictions,
  or an additional confirmatory test chosen after inspection. The rule was an interval criterion.
  Also (D053): “most of SS Cyg's predictability lies in its recent rhythm” (three frozen models do
  not decompose all predictability; say timing history made the larger contribution among them);
  the doubling effect equated with the short/long difference (no class means in C04's evidence);
  the state hypothesis said to entail a positive coefficient (the sign restriction tests CM92's
  reported direction).
- **Size in clock units (C04):** with timing history fixed, doubling duration multiplies M3's
  untruncated median clock by 1.088 (about 9%). This describes exp(mu), not the survival-conditioned
  remaining wait or an observed waiting-time contrast.
- **Measurement qualification:** the source-blind new waveform failed benchmark qualification
  before X4 recovery was scored. X4 passed its conditional screen on a waveform family known
  during development. Do not call the unperformed unseen-family recovery a failed X4 test, or
  call the screen evidence of complete event recovery in the real archive.
- **Practical utility:** the paired likelihood gain does not measure telescope-scheduling benefit
  or the fraction of a physical disc state revealed by duration. Neither quantity was tested.

## Predeclared secondary comparisons (C06–C11)

- **Eras (C06, C07):** the estimates are descriptive. The early segment overlaps the CM92 record,
  while the later segment is outside the published duration–gap sign analyses known to this
  project. The later gain, 0.0057 [−0.0107,0.0221], is inconclusive. Era intervals overlap; there
  was no prespecified era-difference test. Claim neither disappearance nor independent replication.
- **Type/within-type rule (C08):** its frozen gain, 0.0025 [−0.0022,0.0072], is inconclusive. The
  nonnegative uncentred type component is zero after short outbursts. The whole rule also has a
  centred within-type term, and can shorten some short-outburst forecasts. C22 explores a shared
  level adjustment; it does not replace C08 or establish a validated corrected type forecast.
- **Sensitivities (C09):** uniform-end marks, previous-gap-only baseline, two-day delay,
  joint-valid rows and three-year clusters give positive lower bounds. These probe the same
  comparison and are not additional independent confirmations.
- **Timing baseline (C10):** M2's gain over M0 is non-trivial. It does not establish that M2 is the
  best activity model or eliminate latent-state explanations.
- **Calibration (C11):** the realised sample has overcoverage: the nominal central intervals
  cover more observations than their nominal fractions. The ranges are partial-identification
  bounds conditional on extracted onset brackets and censoring, over all scored issuances.
  They are neither confidence intervals for population coverage nor guaranteed coverage of true
  physical onsets. Selected-onset mid-CDF deciles are descriptive; no uniformity test is claimed.

## Evaluation-only association diagnostics (C12, C13)

The signed offsets are fitted after the result, with evaluation M2 refitted and held fixed. Their
LR support intervals do not propagate baseline, dependence or measurement uncertainty.

The separate negative lag coefficient is **compatible with** alternation and a positive current-mark
association. Its origin is not causally decomposed. The joint design is correlated but full rank,
with a modest condition number of 1.79. Both conditional support intervals include zero; correlation
alone does not explain that result or establish coefficient nonidentifiability. These timings do
not identify a physical mechanism. A negative net lag can coexist with a positive slow component;
A10k supplies an analytic counterexample, not an empirical rival fit.

## Exploratory post-result calculations (C20–C25)

All are explicitly labelled **exploratory, post-result**. Refits and evaluation-fitted adjustments
reuse the evaluation outcomes. Their descriptive intervals condition on those choices rather than
propagate all their estimation uncertainty. None supplies another held-out confirmation.

- **Global recentering and level shifts (C20):** shifting M2 by the frozen mark's evaluation mean
  contribution gives about 0.00370 nats; globally recentering that mark gives a 0.01283-nat score
  contrast. The two orderings differ by about 0.000012 nats, so this is an approximately additive,
  ordering-dependent description. It is not a causal partition. A single global mean does not
  remove drift within the evaluation period. Avoid “genuine/drift-free within-period information”.
  The best evaluation log-location shift reduces the original untruncated clock scale by about
  4.57%; it does not directly estimate empirical forecast bias or the conditional remaining wait.
- **Duration classes (C21):** with historical M2 nuisance held fixed and a free level, the
  type-only model reaches a larger in-sample likelihood than linear log duration (LR 14.7 versus
  11.7 against level only). Those equally dimensional models are nonnested. Adding linear
  within-type duration changes the likelihood little (LR 0.17); no equivalence/absence test was
  performed. This is compatible with a parsimonious class interpretation, not proof that all
  predictive information resides in type. The development fits were similar, not proven equivalent.
  Exponentiating the type coefficient gives about a 14% ratio of **untruncated working-clock
  scales**, conditional on model covariates. It is not directly an observed mean-gap difference or
  a remaining-wait contrast. “About five days” is permissible only as an explicitly chosen
  illustration at a 36-day clock scale, not an empirical gap estimate.
- **Shared level adjustment (C22):** the frozen type-plus-within rule gains about 0.00905 nats
  when both models receive M2's evaluation-fitted common level. This shows sensitivity to coding
  and baseline calibration. It does not uniquely explain the original weak score or rescue C08.
  The restriction concerns the type component alone. Future fixed-baseline contrasts should define
  indicator coding and intercept freedom together; centring is not a universal rule for all models.
- **Era-specific type refits (C23):** conditional fit estimates, without a temporal-change test.
- **Alternation (C24):** these are positive-duration pairs at forecast issuances, including one
  repeat-flagged record, not a catalog of independent unique consecutive outbursts. The joint
  model's subset is different. Alternation is prior art. Negative marginal/lag relationships do
  not by themselves exclude a dominant slow driver coexisting with a stronger alternating component.
- **Block and SE diagnostics (C25):** eight block means are positive; leave-one-block-out
  **means** stay positive. The strict rule fails at an SE multiplier of about 1.42705: a 42%
  inflation still passes, an exact 43% does not. This arithmetic holds the estimate, t critical
  value and block definition fixed. It does not validate dependence, compensate every uncertainty
  source, or show that every omission retains the original interval decision.

## Prior art, novelty and future data (C40, C41)

- **CM92 (C40):** Claude re-read Table 4 and the Fig. 5 subscript conventions on the original scan
  (A10l). The quiescence after an outburst correlates with its duration at r = 0.223 and the
  quiescence before it at 0.282; successive durations at −0.400; 401 of 705 durations fall below
  12 d. CM92 read the positive successive-quiescence correlations as a hint of secular variation.
  Allowed: “CM92 reported the association, the alternation and the split”. Not allowed: “we
  discover”, or attributing to CM92 a forecast or a test of duration beyond the preceding quiescence.
- **Novelty (C41):** one prior held-out timing forecast exists: Grigorievskiy et al. (2014) scored
  point forecasts of outburst onset for V363 Lyr from Kepler photometry, with a single test outburst.
  Allowed: “to our knowledge, the first preregistered, frozen-parameter probabilistic evaluation of
  dwarf-nova recurrence forecasts on held-out data, and the first held-out test of whether the completed
  outburst's duration adds forecast skill beyond a specified timing-history baseline”. Not allowed: “the first out-of-sample forecast of
  dwarf-nova outbursts”, or any unqualified priority claim. C41 is a supported statement about a bounded independent search, not universal priority.
  No ADS export was available; observer/non-English coverage and some full texts remain incomplete.
- **Physical reading (Cannizzo 1993):** in that disc-instability model tuned to SS Cyg, long
  outbursts accrete a larger share of the disc than short ones (about 30% against 3% at the model's
  default resolution, falling as resolution rises), and the depleted outer disc tends to make the
  next outburst short: an account of alternation. Outbursts start near the inner edge, so model
  quiescent intervals follow the quiescent viscous drift time. Separately, moving averages of the
  real light curve show longer recurrence times where the long-to-short ratio is higher, which the
  model reproduces through slow parameter changes. Allowed: the forecast gain is **compatible
  with** a cycle-to-cycle disc-mass account and with duration acting as a proxy for a slowly
  varying state that the recent-gap level does not fully capture. Not allowed: “confirms the disc
  instability model”, “demonstrates disc-mass memory”, or a prediction attributed to the 1993
  model that it did not make (Claude's reading found no reported quiescence contrast after long
  versus short outbursts).
