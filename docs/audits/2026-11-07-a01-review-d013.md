## Source and numerical check

I retrieved the original [CM92 ADS scan](https://articles.adsabs.harvard.edu/pdf/1992ApJ...401..642C)
and inspected Table 4 on printed page 645 (PDF page 4). It gives the duration–
following-gap, duration–preceding-gap and adjacent-gap correlations as 0.223,
0.282 and 0.382. Figure 5 defines the interval directions. These are published,
linear-scale sample correlations, not correlations estimated by this project.

With X = duration, Y = following gap and Z = preceding gap,

```
r_XY.Z = (0.223 - 0.282*0.382) / sqrt((1-0.282^2)*(1-0.382^2))
       = 0.130012289001705
g = -0.5*log(1-r_XY.Z^2) = 0.008523842417362 nats
4/g = 469.271932086922
```

The Gaussian gain follows by comparing the correctly specified Gaussian
conditional distributions: the residual variance ratio is `1-r^2`, so the
expected log-density gain is half its negative log. It describes ideal
conditional information under those assumptions. It is not an upper bound,
a lognormal effect estimate or a validated prior for M3 versus M2. More
conditioning variables can either reduce or increase a partial correlation.

## Why 470 is not a powered forecast comparison

`2*n*g` reaches approximately 8 at n=470. An expected known-model log-density
ratio is not a sampling distribution or a detection guarantee. For a fitted
regression LR, the null statistic is also random and its expectation is not
zero. Sample size requires a test, error rates, desired power and dependence
assumptions.

Two deliberately different iid illustrations make the distinction explicit:

| Hypothetical n | Ideal partial-regression power, two-sided alpha=0.005 | Known-parameter paired-score power, one-sided alpha=0.05 (normal approximation) |
|---:|---:|---:|
| 55 | 0.0290 | 0.1233 |
| 400 | 0.4179 | 0.3693 |
| 470 | 0.5058 | 0.4116 |

For the regression reference, use one additional predictor and one control,
`df=n-3`, noncentrality `r*sqrt(df/(1-r^2))`, and the noncentral-t tail beyond
the null critical value. This is an iid Gaussian planning calculation, not
exact power for the planned historical analysis.

For the paired-score reference, let independent X,E be standard normal,
`v=1-r^2`, and `Y=r*X+sqrt(v)*E`. Compare the true conditional density
`Y|X ~ N(rX,v)` with `Y ~ N(0,1)`. The individual score difference is
`d=g+(Y^2-E^2)/2`, with `E[d]=g` and `Var(d)=r^2`. Its mean-test normal
approximation has power `Phi(g*sqrt(n)/abs(r)-1.6448536)`; an 80% planning
solution is approximately 1,438 independent forecasts. This is a counterexample
to transferring regression power to forecast-score power, not our required
sample size. The two columns use different tests and significance levels and
should not be compared as rankings of efficiency.

The real rolling/frozen forecast pipeline must determine its own score-difference
distribution under serial dependence, fitting error, observation uncertainty,
censoring and competing baselines. No forecast count has yet been measured.
A small evaluation block may be informative but cannot be promised to confirm
sign or calibration. Low power does not mean a detection is impossible.

## Other proposals to carry forward carefully

- **Novelty:** retain “no matching evaluation found in the documented search”.
  “Nobody has answered” exceeds the search, especially with the 2014 full text
  unresolved. The access gaps do not prevent an initial data-feasibility audit.
- **M1:** type, type plus within-type duration, and continuous duration are
  reasonable development candidates. Bimodality does not select the best
  forecast representation. Freeze the choice on the open data and simulations.
- **M2:** recent type fractions and quiescent brightness expand the original
  timing-only information set. Name that baseline explicitly and distinguish
  it from timing-only M2. If testing the focal completed mark's incremental
  value, do not include that same mark inside M2's recent-type statistic.
  Symbolic-duration memory over ten cycles does not establish a mandatory
  ten-lag quiescent-gap autoregression.
- **Endpoint error:** under independent centered error of variance v,
  `Corr(D+delta,Q-delta)=(Cov(D,Q)-v)/sqrt((Var(D)+v)*(Var(Q)+v))`.
  The denominator changes too. Double-annotation disagreement cannot identify
  shared bias without additional assumptions. The proposed 0.03 threshold is
  a sensitivity candidate, not a validated Gate C criterion or permission to
  ignore endpoint uncertainty. Use injection/recovery and joint boundary
  propagation to establish its practical consequences.

The research question remains incremental prediction for a named, selected
system. One SS Cyg acquisition is the next empirical step; a single-star
paper and any generalization remain conditional on feasibility and precision.
