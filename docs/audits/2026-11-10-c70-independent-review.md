## What the reported fade lag measures

The actual statistic is the **last raw support time of the first selected
decisive-faint visit minus the closure time of the last threshold-bright
visit**. The bright visit need not be decisive-bright, and the faint visit can
contain a magnitude limit. Neither endpoint is an independently known physical
threshold crossing. The table pools all pre-1990 clock-available raw features,
not just 1980s features.

| Raw-route statistic | SS Cyg median | X Leo median |
|---|---:|---:|
| Reported visit-clock lag, all pre-1990 | 1.7038 d | 3.8210 d |
| Same lag, issues in the 1980s only | 1.4400 d | 3.7920 d |
| Latest threshold-bright raw support to faint anchor, all pre-1990 | 1.8538 d | 3.9710 d |

Replacing last-bright visit closure with its latest supporting raw bright
report adds 0.15 d at the 10th, 50th and 90th percentiles for every target and
SS Cyg. This reconstructs the clock convention; it is not a physical timing
error estimate. Period differences can be substantial: SS Aur's reported
median is 4.73 d over its whole record but 1.95 d for 1980s issues.

X Leo's fully supported previous-gap median is **5.13 d**, versus SS Cyg's
33.59 d. Its onset-window median is **2.387 d** among all interval outcomes,
and its visit-clock fade lag is 3.821 d among clock-available features. Those
are different selected populations; adding their marginal medians is not a
gap decomposition. The 12 nonpositive latest-gap labels correspond to **12
distinct gap IDs**, so this count is not repeated credit to one gap.

The SS Cyg comparison shows that retained yield differs by target under these
rules. It does not establish that the rules are not strict, or uniquely
attribute the difference to faint quiescence, visual depth or cadence. A
descriptive statement about measured support losses and timing conventions is
supported; physical-cause and all-model impossibility statements are not.
