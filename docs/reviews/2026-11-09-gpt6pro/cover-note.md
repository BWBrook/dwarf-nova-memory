## Three issues need resolution first

### 1. The score needs an explicit observation-model interpretation

The onset brackets are **estimated uncertainty intervals** derived from the extraction procedure. They are not automatically equivalent to ordinary censoring intervals known to contain the true event time.

Assigning probability to those brackets produces a well-defined comparison statistic. Calling it a proper predictive likelihood for physical onset requires an additional argument about how the observations produce the brackets. Giving both models the same brackets does not, by itself, establish that argument.

This also affects the interpretation of the calibration figure: compatibility with an estimated bracket is not automatically coverage of the true onset.

I have **not** demonstrated that this reverses the model ranking or invalidates the numerical gain. The immediate remedy is to define the working score and its assumptions precisely and bring the headline claims into alignment. A stronger physical-outcome claim requires stronger measurement-model support.

### 2. Shared-endpoint information remains a substantive alternative explanation

I independently reproduced the project’s counterexample showing that measured duration can improve the forecast even when **true duration never varies**. The positive gain survives a fixed baseline and a nonnegative duration coefficient.

That construction is deliberately artificial; it is not an estimate of the artefact in SS Cyg. Its significance is logical: the sign constraint does not rule out information created by endpoint error and landmark selection.

The existing endpoint sensitivity and delayed-issue analysis do not isolate this pathway. The report specifies a bounded next diagnostic: retain matched synthetic forecast opportunities, score the same forecasts against the true next onset, and then examine a properly trained oracle-endpoint comparison. The possible outcomes—and their limits—are spelled out.

### 3. There is a concrete censoring branch to audit

Both evidence builders can classify an onset bracket crossing a censoring cutoff as survival to that cutoff.

For example, an estimated onset interval **(18, 22]** with a cutoff at **20** becomes “right-censored at 20”, although onset at 19 remains consistent with the interval.

**The code path is demonstrated. Its occurrence in the actual evaluation is not established.** The public summaries identify six censored forecasts—four coverage censors, one supersession censor and one record-end censor—but do not expose the candidate brackets needed to settle this.

## How I would expand it

I would not describe the current paper as salami slicing: it asks one coherent question. But I agree with the concern behind your additional instruction. **A small score increment for one star over one baseline leaves too much of the interesting scientific question unresolved to make procedural rigour alone carry a broad-impact claim.**

The larger question I recommend is:

> **Which properties of a completed dwarf-nova outburst provide reproducible information about recurrence beyond evolving timing history, and how much of that apparent information survives an explicit treatment of observation and event measurement?**

The report develops three connected work packages.

### A. Establish what is observable and scoreable

Resolve the bracket-score and censoring questions, then distinguish gains attributable to the extracted outcome, the uncertain clock origin and the underlying event target.

A blinded boundary comparison on already inspected real data would complement the synthetic work. It should be treated as a measurement sensitivity, with uncertain annotations—not as unquestionable ground truth.

### B. Separate changing activity from additional outburst information

Use a small, interpretable model sequence: the existing benchmark, a model that adapts its recurrence level, an evolving-state model, and matched additions of completed-outburst properties.

One important design detail is what enters the state estimate. If the baseline already uses completed duration to infer its state, adding a duration coefficient answers a narrower question. If only the duration model can adapt its state, adaptation and duration are confounded.

The aim should be to determine what predictive information the marks add. Distinguishing causal disc mechanisms requires further identifiable predictions.

### C. Test transfer across comparable systems

Begin with a modest development cohort in a comparable ordinary-outburst regime, selected by classification and observation adequacy. Determine the eventual validation cohort through explicit eligibility rules and precision planning.

The existing population catalogues are useful for that design, but they are not ready-made collections of accurately measured consecutive cycles. Their treatment of missed events and heterogeneous photometry reinforces the need to make observation modelling central to the expansion. [arxiv.org](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1512.03821?utm_source=chatgpt.com)

A stronger paper could establish a reproducible mark increment, show that much apparent predictability is observational, or demonstrate that the relationship depends on activity regime. Each would be informative if established with useful precision. An inconclusive result would remain inconclusive.

The report includes stages and stopping rules so that this expansion does not become an unlimited programme of additional models and stars. **It does not require opening the 2018-onward archive.**
